# Clinical measures used alongside NCS in a neck-pain study

## Neck Disability Index (NDI)
The NDI has ten sections (pain intensity, personal care, lifting, reading, headaches, concentration, work, driving, sleeping, recreation), each scored 0-5, for a total of 50. It is commonly expressed as a percentage. Conventional bands on the 50-point scale are: 0-4 no disability, 5-14 mild, 15-24 moderate, 25-34 severe and 35 or more complete disability. When a section is not applicable, the percentage is computed over the answered sections.

## IPAQ short form
The International Physical Activity Questionnaire short form records days and minutes of vigorous activity, moderate activity and walking over the last seven days, plus sitting time. Weekly MET-minutes are computed using 8.0, 4.0 and 3.3 METs for vigorous, moderate and walking. Bouts under 10 minutes are excluded and daily minutes are truncated at 180. Category High requires vigorous activity on at least 3 days achieving 1500 MET-min/week, or 7 days of any combination achieving 3000. Category Moderate requires 3 days of 20 minutes vigorous, or 5 days of 30 minutes moderate or walking, or 5 days of any combination achieving 600 MET-min/week. Others are Low. Prolonged sitting is an independent risk marker for neck pain.

## Body composition
Bioelectrical impedance reports body fat percentage, fat-free mass, skeletal muscle mass, visceral fat level and total body water. For Asian populations, WHO Asia-Pacific BMI cut-offs are used: below 18.5 underweight, 18.5-22.9 normal, 23-24.9 overweight and 25 or more obese. Higher adiposity and a larger wrist index are associated with carpal tunnel syndrome, and obesity has been associated with slower conduction and lower SNAP amplitudes, so body composition is a relevant covariate when analysing NCS parameters.
