# Ulnar and radial neuropathies

## Ulnar neuropathy
The most common site is the elbow (cubital tunnel or retro-epicondylar groove), followed by the wrist (Guyon canal). Electrodiagnostic features are focal slowing of motor conduction across the elbow (a drop of more than about 10 m/s compared with the forearm segment), conduction block, a reduced ulnar SNAP to digit V and reduced ADM or FDI CMAP. A reduced ulnar SNAP separates ulnar neuropathy (post-ganglionic) from C8 radiculopathy, where the ulnar SNAP is preserved. The dorsal ulnar cutaneous sensory response is spared in lesions at the wrist.

## Radial neuropathy
Radial nerve lesions at the spiral groove give wrist drop with low radial CMAP and reduced superficial radial SNAP. Posterior interosseous neuropathy spares the superficial radial SNAP. Isolated superficial radial sensory neuropathy (from tight bands or handcuffs) produces a low superficial radial SNAP with normal motor studies. A normal superficial radial SNAP in the presence of weakness of radial-innervated muscles points to C7 radiculopathy instead.
