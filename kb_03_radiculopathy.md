# Cervical radiculopathy: electrodiagnostic localisation

## The pre-ganglionic principle
The dorsal root ganglion lies in the intervertebral foramen. Cervical disc herniation and foraminal stenosis usually compress the root proximal to (pre-ganglionic to) the dorsal root ganglion. The sensory cell body and its peripheral axon remain intact, so the SNAP is preserved even when the patient has sensory loss. This is the key feature distinguishing radiculopathy from plexopathy or peripheral neuropathy, where the lesion is post-ganglionic and SNAPs are reduced.

## Expected NCS findings
In radiculopathy, sensory studies in the affected dermatome are normal. Motor studies are usually normal; CMAP amplitude falls only when axonal loss in the root is substantial, and F-wave latencies may be prolonged or show increased side-to-side difference. For C8 and T1 radiculopathy, the ulnar CMAP (ADM) and median CMAP (APB) may be low, while the ulnar SNAP (digit V) is preserved. For C6 and C7, the median SNAP to digits I-III and the radial SNAP remain normal.

## Role of NCS in a neck-pain study
NCS in cervical radiculopathy is primarily used to exclude mimics (carpal tunnel syndrome, ulnar neuropathy, brachial plexopathy, polyneuropathy) and to show the SNAP-sparing pattern. Needle EMG of myotomal muscles and paraspinals is more sensitive for radiculopathy. A normal NCS does not exclude a radiculopathy, and abnormal NCS in a patient with neck pain may reflect a coexisting entrapment (the so-called double crush).
