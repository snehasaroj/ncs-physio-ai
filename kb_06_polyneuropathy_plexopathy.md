# Polyneuropathy and brachial plexopathy

## Generalised polyneuropathy
Polyneuropathy is suggested when abnormalities involve several nerves in more than one limb, typically symmetrically and in a length-dependent pattern. Axonal forms (for example diabetes, alcohol, B12 deficiency) show reduced SNAP amplitudes first, then reduced CMAPs, with mild slowing. Demyelinating forms show marked slowing, prolonged distal and F-wave latencies, conduction block and dispersion. Lower-limb (sural, peroneal) studies are needed to confirm a length-dependent polyneuropathy; when only upper-limb nerves are studied, bilateral multi-nerve abnormalities should prompt screening.

## Brachial plexopathy
Plexus lesions are post-ganglionic, so SNAPs in the affected territory are reduced together with CMAPs. Upper trunk lesions reduce median (digit I) and lateral antebrachial cutaneous SNAPs; lower trunk lesions reduce ulnar and medial antebrachial cutaneous SNAPs. Combined sensory and motor loss in one limb without a single-nerve explanation should raise suspicion of plexopathy, including neurogenic thoracic outlet syndrome.
