# F-waves

## Physiology
The F-wave is a late motor response produced when an antidromic impulse travels up the motor axon to the anterior horn cell, back-fires a small proportion of motor neurons, and returns orthodromically to the muscle. It therefore tests conduction along the entire motor pathway, including the proximal segments (plexus and ventral root) that routine studies cannot reach directly.

## Interpretation
The minimal F-wave latency is the most reliable measure. It depends on limb length or height, so a fixed cut-off (about 31-32 ms for median and ulnar nerves at the wrist in adults of average height) should ideally be adjusted for height. A side-to-side difference greater than about 2 ms is also suspicious. Prolonged F-waves with normal distal conduction suggest a proximal lesion such as a radiculopathy or plexopathy, but sensitivity for single-root radiculopathy is limited because a muscle is innervated by more than one root and the delay is diluted over a long pathway. Absent or persistently delayed F-waves occur in demyelinating polyneuropathies such as Guillain-Barre syndrome.
