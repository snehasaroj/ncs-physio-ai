# Median neuropathy at the wrist (carpal tunnel syndrome)

## Pathophysiology
Compression of the median nerve under the transverse carpal ligament causes focal demyelination first, followed by axonal loss in severe cases. Sensory fibres are affected before motor fibres.

## Electrodiagnostic findings
Early: prolonged median sensory peak latency and slowed sensory velocity across the wrist with a normal ulnar sensory study in the same hand. Moderate: additional prolongation of the median motor distal latency to APB. Severe: reduced or absent median SNAP and reduced APB CMAP amplitude. Comparison studies (median versus ulnar or radial latency to the same digit, palmar mixed studies) increase sensitivity; a median-ulnar sensory latency difference greater than about 0.5 ms is suggestive.

## Relevance to neck pain
Carpal tunnel syndrome is a frequent mimic of C6-C7 radiculopathy because both cause hand paraesthesia. The two can coexist. Normal ulnar and radial studies help confirm the focal nature of median involvement.
